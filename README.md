## About
This project is an application that shows how interconnected economies support each other, delaying economic crises, but ultimately make them worse. This is a theme shown in “The Lexus and the Olive Tree” by Thomas Friedman. My piece shows the first part of this theme because interdependent nodes stay uncollapsed (not removed) for longer, a maximum of 13 waves, whereas an unconnected node will collapse after 2 waves. My piece shows the second part of this theme because a collapse will trigger the collapse of the entire network it is connected to.

## Primer
This application shows the effects of economic crises on interconnected economies through two mechanics. The mechanics are:
1. [Networks are stronger](#Networks-are-stronger)
2. [Networks fall together](#Networks-fall-together)

### Networks are stronger
Networks are stronger together, both in the application and in the real world. Some examples are:
- Unprecedented development after World War II because of global trade networks and advancements in communication after the war
- South Asian industry developing rapidly because of cheap access to global oil and trade networks

### Networks fall together
Networks fall together in the application, as in real life. A couple of examples of this are:
- Great Depression
- South Asian Financial Crisis
- Russian Financial Crisis
- 2008 Financial Crisis

All of these crises started in a segment of the global network, and expanded to encompass the majority of it. For example, the SA Financial Crisis caused the Russian Financial Crisis, and the Great Depression was caused by a local American depression, that expanded to encompass much of the developed world.
